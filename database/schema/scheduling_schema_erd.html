Scheduling schema — planned shifts vs. actual worked shifts

Border style carries the ownership rule. Dashed tables are projections, rebuilt by replaying the event stream. Solid tables are scheduling-owned, and a replay must never touch them — a Humanity id living on a projection would be wiped by a rebuild, and the next publish run would create every shift in Humanity a second time. Four tables are omitted here for legibility and are in the Mermaid version alongside this file: employee_store_assignments, employee_positions, event_inbox, scheduling_outbox_events. The two nullable pointers employees.primary_store_id and employees.primary_position_id are likewise drawn only in the Mermaid version, where they don't cross the diagram.

Entity-relationship diagram. SHIFTS holds the planned schedule and WORK_SEGMENTS holds actual worked hours from TCP, joined by a nullable shift_id foreign key with ON DELETE SET NULL. EMPLOYEES is a flat projection of the hiring employee event. EMPLOYEE_REQUESTS with EMPLOYEE_REQUEST_DECISIONS records time off and cover requests and who decided them. EMPLOYEE_PAY_HISTORIES carries hourly rates for labour-cost estimates. store_settings and integration_identities are scheduling-owned and carry no foreign keys.

Three deliberate nulls. shifts.employee_id — an open shift, placed before anyone is assigned. work_segments.time_out — an open punch, which has no hours to approve and so blocks the day close as its own category. work_segments.shift_id — unscheduled cover. It is ON DELETE SET NULL: deleting a plan must never delete the record of hours somebody worked.

A split shift is two rows, not one row with a gap. Both carry the same split_group_id and differ by split_part. One row stays one continuous block, so it maps 1:1 onto a Humanity shift and 1:1 onto the punches that reconcile against it — publishing, matching and editing need no special case. The gap between parts is implied by part 1's end_at and part 2's start_at, and is not unpaid_break_minutes: a break is time inside one block, a split gap is time between two. A split and a lunch break produce the same two punches; what tells them apart is whether those punches point at two shift rows or one.

Availability is a concrete hour range. One row is one window — Monday 16:00 to 21:00 — and several rows per day are expected, which is exactly what a split shift is validated against. An overnight window is encoded by column order rather than a flag that could contradict it: available_to < available_from wraps past midnight, and day_of_week always names the day the window started on.

birth_date, not age. Age is derived and wrong on every employee's birthday, and it cannot answer the question that matters — minor labour rules turn on age on the date of the shift. Upstream it lives on employee_obsessions, not employees.

Not projected, though the hiring payload carries it: race, religion, t-shirt size, photo path, street address, emergency contacts. Race and religion in particular have no place in the system that decides who works when. ssn and bank account details are hidden upstream and never arrive.

Pay is here only to cost a schedule. base_pay and performance_pay are hourly, kept as history so a past week is costed at the rate that applied then. The estimate is computed in the query that renders the week, never cached on shifts. Nothing at the database level protects these rows — gate them in the application.
